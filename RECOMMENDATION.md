# Recommendation

**Operational recommendation (2-3 sentences):**
For 29 October to 7 November 2026, our forecast puts all 15 Lahore areas in the *unhealthy but not hazardous* band (103–164 µg/m³). Levels peak on Fridays and are highest in Walled City (155 average), DHA (150) and Shadman (149), so authorities should keep sensitive-group advisories active every day (DOC-01: reduce prolonged outdoor exertion), with no school half-day, odd-even or construction trigger currently forecast (DOC-03, DOC-05, DOC-08). Because hazardous days in this data are sudden single-sensor spikes that no input predicts, authorities should check same-day sensor readings each evening before school decisions (DOC-03 already decides the evening before). They should also treat "not hazardous" as a forecast, not a guarantee, especially for days forecast above 145 µg/m³: in validation, the share of such days that turned out hazardous was 6–17%, compared with 2–4% for lower forecasts.

**Limitations (2-3 bullet points):**
- **Hazardous alarm recall is 0.** We apply the SPEC rule (`hazardous = predicted_pm25 >= 165`), and no forecast reaches 165, so 0 of 150 rows are flagged. Spikes (≈ 3.4% of sensor-days) are independent of weekday, sensor, neighbouring sensors and weather. Of the 8 alarm methods we tested (including classifiers and quantile models), none predicts them better than about 2× chance (PR-AUC 0.09 vs 0.03). A calibrated lower alarm (≥ 145–150) would catch more hazardous days at the cost of many false alarms; the trade-off is quantified in SPRINT1.md §8.4 and the team chose SPEC consistency.
- **Forecasts are 1–10 days ahead, not next-day.** Observations end on 2026-10-28, so the holdout's later "forecast origins" have no data behind them. Every row is forecast from 28 October with a direct multi-horizon model. Validation error grows only slowly with horizon (MAE 12.3 at 1 day vs 14.1 at 10 days), but day-10 rows are less certain than true next-day forecasts.
- **Short history, no peak season.** 120 days (Jul–Oct) do not include November's peak smog, so any rise beyond the October trend (≈ +0.6 µg/m³/day) is not learned. The model beats the 7-day-average baseline by 16% (MAE 13.2 vs 15.7 µg/m³, walk-forward validation), but that accuracy is measured on Jul–Oct conditions, not on peak-season extremes.
